Every Account On One Page.Pace Checked Every 5 Minutes.

Two Jobs, One App.Account Lookup And Pace.

It runs entirely on the account manager's own Mac, as a small native window. Nothing is hosted, and there is no cloud copy.

Job 1 · Account Lookup

Any Account, One Page

Search any GOAL account and get its status, contacts, meetings, tasks, notes, the last meeting's recap, the sales handoff and the reporting site. Ask questions about one account or the whole book.

Job 2 · Pace

Same-Day Warning On Spend And Conversion

Budget pace and click-to-lead checks every 5 minutes, with alerts, a morning briefing, best hours and a Sources review. Pace is the engine of the Croissant Chrome extension (v2.9.2), now running inside the app.

Terms Used On This Page
Account manager (AM)
The GOAL rep who looks after a book of agency accounts. Each copy of the app shows one AM's book.
Pace
How much of today's budget an account has spent, against where it normally is by this time of day.
Click-to-lead
The share of clicks that become leads. A sudden drop often means a broken form, landing page or call routing.
GOAL sign-in
The AM's own logged-in session on the GOAL admin portal. It lasts 12 hours. It is not an API key.
"Log in as"
The GOAL admin feature that opens a client's account as its owner, with no client password. The app uses it to read budgets.
GOAL Connector
A small Chrome extension that passes the sign-in from the GOAL page to the app on the same Mac.
Source bid multiplier
The bid adjustment for one traffic source on a campaign. It is the only thing the app can change in GOAL.
Quote Funnel, Lead Marketplace
GOAL's two campaign types. Quote Funnel budgets are in dollars. Marketplace limits are in units.

Which Accounts

One AM's Book

Accounts that are Active in GOAL with that AM as Account Manager. Onboarding and Paused accounts are read by name only.

What It Reads

Totals, Not Leads

Spend, clicks, lead counts, call counts and budgets. No individual leads, so no consumer PII comes into the app.

How Often

Every 5 Minutes

Today's totals on each check. The account list hourly. Budgets and history once a day.

How Long

A 12-Hour Pass

The sign-in expires after 12 hours. The app then shows disconnected until a GOAL page is open again.

How It Pulls GOAL Data For One AM's Accounts
  1. The AM logs in to the GOAL admin portal in Chrome, as they do now.
  2. The Connector hands over the sign-in. It pairs with the app on the same Mac and keeps the sign-in for the browser session only. The app saves it in an encrypted file.
  3. The app works out who the AM is from the sign-in, then asks GOAL for accounts that are Active with that AM as Account Manager. It is the same list as the admin Clients page filtered to Active and that AM.
  4. Onboarding and Paused accounts are read by name only, so they link to their pages before launch. They show no pace numbers and raise no alerts until Active.
  5. Every 5 minutes, or on Refresh, it sends at least two analytics requests for today's totals. Some checks add Marketplace, source and hourly reads.
  6. Every hour it re-reads the account list, and once a day it pulls 14 days of history.
  7. Once a day it reads budgets, on the first check after midnight Pacific. It signs in as each account's owner, as "log in as" does, reads the active campaigns' budgets and changes nothing. The owner session stays in memory and is never saved. Failed reads retry 15, 30, 60 and 120 minutes later.
  8. It compares spend with budget and history, updates the screen and raises alerts. After 12 hours the sign-in expires and the app shows disconnected until a GOAL page is open again.

How The App Reads GOAL Data.What It Touches. What Is Exposed.

From the data access review of 11 Nov 2026. An independent check of the code confirmed or corrected each claim, with one live test session.

The Short Version

It Has No Access Of Its Own

The app borrows the GOAL admin sign-in of whoever is logged in to the admin portal in Chrome on that Mac. Without an admin login it gets nothing.

The Sign-In Is A 12-Hour Pass

GOAL's own admin site keeps it in the browser. It is the AM's login session, and it expires. It is not a permanent API key.

The App Asks For Totals Only

Spend, clicks, lead counts, call counts and budgets for one AM's accounts. It never asks for individual leads, so no consumer PII comes into the app.

The Sign-In Can Do More Than The App Does

It is not limited to one AM's accounts, and it can sign in as any client's owner without their password. The app does that to read budgets. Misused outside the app, the sign-in could reach whatever a client sees in their portal.

The Method Came From Croissant

The Croissant team build (v2.9.2) reads the same sign-in and signs in as account owners the same way. It keeps the sign-in unencrypted in Chrome and has fewer checks around bid changes.

The Real Exposure Is The Admin Login

Any admin user with an AI coding tool can do what this app does. The app adds a local copy of the data, a faster way to pull it, and a small local service on the Mac that needs a few more locks.

Most fixes add no steps for the user.Several make the app simpler. The strongest ones are on the GOAL side and would close the gap for every tool, not only this one.
What The Sign-In Can Reach, And The Smaller Part The App Uses
What The App Uses

  • The list of users on a client accountTo find the owner's name and email.
  • Signing in as a client's ownerTo read campaigns, budgets, Marketplace totals and source settings.
Reachable, Not Used By The App
  • Totals for any other AM's accountsLikely. One settings field changes the AM number.
  • Lead records in a client's portalLikely, untested. "Log in as" gives the client's own view.
  • Changing budgets, bids or campaign status as the clientLikely, untested. The app refuses these while the bid switch is off.

The gap between the two boxes is the exposure. It exists for every admin login, with or without this app. The "likely" items were not tested, and they should stay untested unless someone approves a check.

What Was Said In Slack, And What The Code Shows

The thread happened before anyone had read how the app works. These are the points that need correcting.

Said In The ThreadWhat Is True
"It figured out a way to extract a bearer API. I didn't ask it to, it came up with this approach."

Nothing was cracked. GOAL's admin site keeps the sign-in in the browser page, where any extension allowed on that page can read it. Croissant v2.9.2 already did this, and the new app reuses the method.

"Bearer API token." "Unique API key."

It is the AM's own login session, good for 12 hours. GOAL did not issue it to an app, and it stops working when it expires.

"One-time setup. Run a command in the console and paste the token into settings."

That was the fallback. The normal route is the GOAL Connector, with no console and no copying. It is also not one-time, because the sign-in expires every 12 hours and the connector passes along the new one.

"It only pulls the accounts tied to the logged-in user."

The app limits itself. The sign-in does not. The app asks for accounts managed by AM number X, taken from the sign-in. A settings field lets you type a different number.

"Pulls data on all the AMs' accounts in real time."

One AM's accounts per copy of the app, checked every 5 minutes by default.

"No PII is exposed in the app."

True for consumer data. The app keeps each account owner's first name and email. And the sign-in it holds could reach lead records if misused outside the app.

"A rep would have to build out their own feature set to get that."

True, and it is not hard. A rep with an admin login and an AI coding tool could do it in an afternoon without this app.

"It requires the app running alongside a custom Chrome extension."

The app needs both. A person misusing an admin login needs neither.

"The other tool uses Claude in Chrome and computer use, with no API involved."

Croissant does use the API. The v2.9.2 team build calls GOAL's API directly with the admin sign-in. A separate tool that clicks through pages would be different.

"This can extract the same reporting data and build the report without any export."

Not yet. Pace pulls pace data today. The 10 to 15 reporting exports are not built.

"If we had a secure API from GOAL it would be easier and safer."

Agreed. It is the best fix on this page.

2

An Internal Bad Actor After Client Data

Likelihood Low To MediumImpact High
2a. With Their Own Admin Login

They need nothing from this app. The steps are to log in, then use the portal or a script. They can already open clients, export reports and log in as a client. An AI coding tool turns that into a bulk pull in an afternoon, and Croissant shows the path is known.

What is missing is a record. Unless GOAL logs bulk reads and "log in as" per admin user, and someone reviews the logs, nobody would notice.

2b. On A Colleague's Mac

They need a colleague's unlocked Mac for a few minutes. They can read and export from the app, copy the project folder to unlock later, or copy the sign-in from the open GOAL tab. Anything they then do in GOAL looks like the colleague did it.

An AM who leaves is a related case. The sign-in dies within 12 hours. The local history, CSV files, cached CRM notes, the Close and Fathom keys and the Notion and email connections stay on that Mac until removed or revoked.

3

Likelihood HighImpact Low

Reducing The Risk.Without Making The App Harder To Use.

Already In Place

  • Listens only on the Mac it runs on. Its GOAL routes refuse requests from other websites.
  • Takes a sign-in only from the first connector that paired with it, or from its own settings page.
  • The part that talks to GOAL can reach GOAL's API and Slack, and nothing else.
  • While the bid switch is off, every change to GOAL is refused. The switch is off by default.
  • The sign-in, Pace data, CRM caches and connections are encrypted on disk.
  • Owner sessions are never written to disk.
  • No lead-level data is requested.
  • A sample-data mode with made-up agencies, so a demo needs no GOAL login.

Company Settings, Set Once

  • Disk encryption and auto-lock required on every Mac that runs it. Work Macs only.
  • A Chrome extension allow-list. This is the best single protection for the GOAL sign-in, with or without this app.
  • An offboarding checklist that revokes the Close, Fathom and AI keys and the Notion and email connections, and removes the app's folder.
  • An agreed position on which AI services may see client data.

GOAL Platform Changes

These need engineering, and they help with every tool.

  • A read-only reporting API with a key per user, limited to that AM's accounts, that can be revoked and rate-limited.
  • An admin-side way to read budgets, so reads never need "log in as".
  • A log of every "log in as" and of bulk reads per admin user, with a review.
  • Keep the admin sign-in out of the page's readable storage, shorten its life and cancel it on sign-out.
  • Limit what each admin role can see through the API.
The Ideas From The Thread, And How Each One Holds Up
Best Option

A Secure API From GOAL

A read-only key per user, limited to that AM's accounts. The app would stop touching the admin sign-in and stop using "log in as".

Moves The Problem

A Middleware Layer

Something still has to sign in to GOAL. A server that does it holds an always-on pass to every account and becomes the target. It only helps when engineering runs it with a read-only account, and at that point it is the secure API.

Fits Reporting

Scheduled Exports

GOAL sends exports to a folder or inbox and the tool reads the files, so the tool holds no GOAL pass at all. The data is a day old. That suits the 10 to 15 reporting exports and does not suit live pace alerts.

Later

Slack-Triggered Reports

These need a bot with data access running on a server, which is the same question as middleware. They make sense once there is an approved data source.

Where It Adds Value.And What Comes Next.

Value Today

  • Same-day warning when an account overspends, underspends or stops converting, across the whole book.
  • A morning briefing for the AM, plus a daily report of yesterday's numbers.
  • One page per account that joins GOAL pace with CRM notes, meetings and email.
  • A working prototype of alerts and reporting that could live inside the product. It can be shown on sample data, with no GOAL login and no real client names.
